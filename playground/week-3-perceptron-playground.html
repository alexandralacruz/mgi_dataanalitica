The AND & XOR Problem Background

Before we start experimenting, let's understand the two problems and what it means for data to be linearly separable.

Linearly Separable

AND Gate

The AND gate outputs 1 only when both inputs are 1. All other combinations produce 0.

A single straight line can separate the green point (class 1) from the red points (class 0). This is what linearly separable means.

NOT Linearly Separable

XOR Gate (Exclusive OR)

XOR outputs 1 when the inputs are different, and 0 when they are the same.

x₁x₂XOR
000
011
101
110

The green points sit at opposite corners. No single straight line can put both green points on one side and both red points on the other. This is NOT linearly separable.

What does "Linearly Separable" mean?

A dataset is linearly separable if there exists a straight line (in 2D), a plane (in 3D), or a hyperplane (in higher dimensions) that perfectly divides the data points into their respective classes.

A perceptron can solve it: If the data IS linearly separable, a single perceptron (one neuron with a step/sigmoid activation) is guaranteed to find a solution. Examples: AND, OR, NAND gates.
A perceptron cannot solve it: If the data is NOT linearly separable, no single perceptron will ever classify all points correctly. Example: XOR. This was the key finding of Minsky & Papert (1969) that led to the "AI winter."
The solution? Neural networks with hidden layers. By combining multiple neurons in layers, we can create non-linear decision boundaries that solve problems like XOR. Use the tabs above to explore this hands-on.
