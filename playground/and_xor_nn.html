XOR Problem Not Linearly Separable

Why a single perceptron cannot solve XOR

A perceptron draws a single straight line to separate two classes. XOR produces a "checkerboard" pattern — class 1 at (0,1) and (1,0), class 0 at (0,0) and (1,1). No single line can correctly separate these four points.

This was first proved by Minsky & Papert in 1969, and it motivated the development of multi-layer networks.

Decision Boundary (MLP)

Perceptron Rule

w ← w + η·(y−ŷ)·x

Works for AND — any linearly separable problem is guaranteed to converge.

MLP Forward Pass

h = σ(W₁x + b₁)
ŷ = σ(W₂h + b₂)

The hidden layer maps inputs to a new space where XOR becomes linearly separable.

Backpropagation

δ = ∂L/∂z
W ← W − η·δ·xᵀ

Chain rule applied layer by layer, propagating the error gradient back through the network.
